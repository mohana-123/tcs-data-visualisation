
## CEO

**1. Revenue concentration by market (demographic lens, explicit)**
Old framing buried "demographic" inside a risk/reward pitch. Fixed: *"Revenue is ~84% concentrated in the UK — is that concentration a reflection of the demographic profile of our current customer base (repeat, established, high-frequency), and does that same profile exist in any other country at meaningful scale?"*
This forces the demographic angle to the surface instead of leaving Country as an implicit variable inside a revenue question.

**2. Product/SKU concentration — keep, add qualitative layer**
Old version was pure "what % of revenue comes from top SKUs." Add: *"...and qualitatively, do the top-selling products share a common theme (price point, category, gift-vs-utility use) that explains why they outperform, or is the concentration arbitrary?"*
Numbers tell you *what's* concentrated; this makes you explain *why*, which is the qualitative half the brief asked for.

**3. Monthly trend / seasonality — keep as is.**
No change needed; it's already appropriately hedged as hypothesis-only.

**4. Customer concentration risk — reframe, don't just quantify**
Weak spot in the original: it only asked "how exposed are we." Fixed: *"How dependent is revenue on the top N customers, and does that concentration reflect a few large accounts we should proactively deepen relationships with, or a structural weakness in customer acquisition?"*
Same data, but forces a strategic interpretation (retain vs. diversify) instead of stopping at a scary percentage.

## CMO

**1. Repeat vs. one-time revenue split — keep as is.**
Solid, foundational, no change needed.

**2. Reorder gap / re-engagement window — keep as is.**
Already correctly framed as an operational marketing question.

**3. Product correlation with repeat buying — this one's genuinely weak, replace framing**
Original implied causation risk even with a disclaimer bolted on. Reframe as explicitly exploratory: *"Which products are most commonly a customer's first purchase, and do customers who start with those products show different repeat behavior — a pattern worth testing, not assuming, as an acquisition/onboarding lever?"*
This is honest about what correlation can and can't tell you, and it's the kind of "we don't fully know, here's what's worth investigating" framing execs actually respect more than false certainty.

**4. Demographic/geographic expansion opportunity — sharpen the demographic framing**
Fixed: *"Outside the UK, which countries show customer behavior (revenue-per-customer, repeat rate) comparable to our best UK segments — i.e., where does the demographic profile suggest marketing spend would replicate what's already working, rather than starting cold?"*
Ties directly back to the brief's "areas that are performing well so they can keep a clear focus on what's working" line, which your original CMO Q4 didn't reference at all.

**What this doesn't fix**: you still only have one demographic variable (Country) to work with in this dataset. If a grader expects segmentation by age/income/gender and this data doesn't have it, no amount of question-rewriting solves that — flag the data limitation in your presentation rather than let the questions imply richer segmentation than the data supports.
